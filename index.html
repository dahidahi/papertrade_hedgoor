Hedged trades on Papertrade, priced

On Papertrade, open a long and a short on the same coin at the same time, then close both. Price direction stops mattering: one side wins, one loses, and you mostly pay the small win-side haircut. The losing side mints PAPER. This shows what that costs per PAPER.

    How this is calculated
    • Both sides open at the same entry price (Hyperliquid BBO mid). There is no spread, funding, slippage or gas charged to you.
    • Winning side: the gain is measured as if the exit were 0.2 bps worse (deadband), then scaled by 0.9 / (1 + 1/(move×15000) + 100000/(10⁶×move×k)) where k is 814.598 for BTC and 483.979 for ETH, then 2% comes off. These are launch values and the owner can change them.
    • Losing side: you pay the raw loss. PAPER mints on that loss (98% of it when the payout queue is empty, 100% when it is active) at the current mint rate.
    • Liquidation: crossing the bust price forfeits the whole margin, and PAPER mints on all of it. Bust is taken as a move of 1 ÷ leverage against that side. The docs put the real bust slightly closer, so liquidation checks here are approximate.
    • Mint rate: 100 per $1 at the start, then 100 × (120M ÷ (120M + H))² once tracked LP passes $2M, where H is the tail progress. It only goes down. Type in the rate your dashboard shows.
    • Cost per PAPER is net cost divided by PAPER minted. It is the PAPER price at which one cycle breaks even.
    • Not modelled: payout-queue delay on the winner's profit (your margin returns at once, the profit can wait), open-interest caps per side, minimum margin and position size, the $10M cap per position, close timing between blocks, owner rule changes, and what PAPER is worth. Repeated cycles assume the same rate each time, though your own losses push it down.
